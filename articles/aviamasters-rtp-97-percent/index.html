What the 97% RTP in Aviamasters Actually Buys You

97% return to player is a long-run average, not a session promise. Here is the sample size it needs, and why an evening can look nothing like it.

By Ryan Murton · Crash games analyst 18 August 2026

🔞 18+ only · Real-money gambling

The 97% figure published for Aviamasters is real, verifiable and almost useless for predicting an evening. It describes what the game returns across millions of rounds — not what your balance does across fifty.

How RTP is measured

Return to player is the total amount paid back divided by the total amount wagered, taken over the game's full simulated history. The house edge is the remaining 3%. Nothing about that figure is per-session, per-player or per-day.

The sample size problem

  • 50 rounds — the result is dominated by whether one high multiplier landed. RTP tells you nothing.

  • 1,000 rounds — a shape starts to appear, still with wide error bars.

  • 100,000+ rounds — the observed return converges on the published figure.

The expected cost scales with total turnover, not with how long you sit there. Doubling the stake doubles the bill, whatever the auto cash-out is set to.

Checking the number yourself

  1. Open the game's info panel — the RTP is stated there by the provider, not the operator.

  2. Compare it with the figure on BGaming's own game page.

  3. If an operator shows a different number, the operator is running a modified configuration. Treat that as a reason to leave.
